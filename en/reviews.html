A Literary Review by Kamran Kouzeh-Kanani

The following review was written by the distinguished literary thinker Kamran Kouzeh-Kanani after reading the short introduction to Part Five, published on this website, concerning the love story of Cratia and Pygmalion.

This text is a masterpiece in portraying the "alchemy of love" and the "transformation of coldness into consciousness."

With remarkable subtlety, the author portrays the contrast between two different worlds. On one side stands Cratia, with her wild, untouched, and mythical beauty, as though she were a fragment of forgotten history and the earthly manifestation of a goddess. She embodies pride and dignity, regarding herself as an impregnable fortress and a forbidden fruit. On the other side stands Pygmalion, an artist confronted with a miracle that neither painting nor sculpture can adequately describe.

What makes this passage profoundly moving is that it transcends superficial love. Faced with Cratia's imposing presence, Pygma lowers his gaze. His reverence and courtesy, rather than creating distance, gradually melt the frozen ocean of her heart. His furtive, respectful glances slowly become a sun of loving warmth, illuminating Cratia's face like the moon.

This is where the author's understanding of love reaches its highest point: love is not an illusory fantasy, but a genuine enchantment, an elixir that sets the current of life flowing through the veins of existence.

The dialogue between these two characters, from the "impregnable fortress" to the "dream of conquering it," represents a romantic contest in which Pygma's passionate words gradually remove the veils of doubt and the weight of Cratia's tribal traditions. The author beautifully demonstrates that, in the presence of authentic love, cultural, urban, and tribal boundaries begin to fade.

The sentence, "The eye through which we behold existence is itself a miracle," expresses the philosophical summit of this passage: beauty is a manifestation of the Creator, and love is the only way to comprehend this miracle.

This work offers a cinematic and poetic portrayal of pride being softened, frozen hearts thawing, and a spiritual love blossoming between two people from different worlds. The author's ability to weave mythology, represented by Pygmalion, together with philosophy and emotion, is truly admirable.

Kamran Kouzeh-Kanani

The Author's Response to the Esteemed Literary Critic

My dear and distinguished Professor Kouzeh-Kanani,

Your literary insight, vivid imagination, and profound understanding of the philosophical and mystical dimensions of this work have astonished me. Indeed, every thoughtful human being is a book yet to be read.

I am deeply pleased and grateful to have a wise and thoughtful friend such as you, someone who has perceived the details of life and the philosophy of love. Your response demonstrates how deeply you have understood the purpose concealed between the lines of this book.

It is an exploration of the progressive stages of understanding love, meaning, and the purpose of life.

A section of this website, which introduces the book in both Persian and English, is devoted to the reflections of thinkers such as yourself, allowing everyone to enjoy the valuable, beautiful, and thought-provoking opinions of others.

Your powerful and profound observations are the first contribution to this international mythic love story. I am delighted that the book has stimulated such deep reflection in you and inspired such a perceptive literary interpretation of a small part of the work.

The depth of your analysis has encouraged me and reaffirmed my belief that the ideas embedded in this book can inspire thoughtful readers to reflect on the meaning of love and existence.

With my best wishes for your health, happiness, and continued success.

Kamran Kouzeh-Kanani's Further Response

A Reflection by the Literary Critic and Reviewer

My dear friend and esteemed author,

Greetings and warm regards.

I have read your kind message several times.

I was merely a reader who placed a shadow of his own understanding on paper in response to the light of your book. If those lines managed to reflect even a small part of the work's depth, the credit belongs to the book itself and to the sincerity of your writing, not to any special ability of mine.

And This Is the Book of Love was not merely a few pages of writing to me.

It was a window into the gradual unfolding of our understanding of love, meaning, and the purpose of life. You weave passion and awareness together so naturally that readers do not merely read the lines; they live them.

I am delighted that a bilingual introduction to this work has been prepared. Devoting a section of the website to the reflections of readers and thinkers is a valuable and enlightening initiative.

I am honored that my note should be the first contribution to this section. I will certainly write about this work with care and affection.

I have also read Seven Beloveds and the Story of Seven Loves with both passion and reflection. In that work:

Love is not merely an emotion; it is knowledge, choice, and responsibility. The relationship between these elements reveals an awakened heart and a capable, expressive pen.

I hope this sincere bond will always remain a guiding light for thought and affection, and that your works will increasingly find their readers and receive the recognition they deserve.

With affection and respect,
Kouzeh-Kanani
22 October 2026

Translator's note: The Persian date given in the original message is 30 Mehr 1405, corresponding to 22 October 2026.
