Passages from the Story of Electra and Pygmalion

Electra: A Symbol of Love and Reason

Pygma had found another teacher of love. Electra was his teacher, guiding him from one station of love to a higher one, through both her outward and inward beauty and through the course of her destiny.

Why was Electra a symbol of love? Perhaps because love, in its entirety, made even the smallest vessel of lust seem insignificant. Electra was reason set ablaze, and being in her company brought immense delight. Love itself is that immense delight which comes to a person only on rare and special occasions.

Here was the answer. Electra had a father born of love and affection, a man who spoke to her of human pain, suffering, sorrow, and loneliness. Through the tears, pain, and suffering of other people, he purified her spirit. He taught her wisdom: in every event, we must search for the cause, and then for the causes behind that cause. When we do so, many problems resolve themselves.

She also had a mother born of love and tenderness. For ten years, while her parents had no other child, Electra was her mother's companion, friend, and comforter. She learned how a sister comes into the world, and how a helpless infant becomes a beautiful and capable being—the most remarkable living creature in terms of reason and love.

She had five angel-like sisters, and four more guardian angels in the form of her maternal and paternal grandparents. They opened before her the world of stories: tales of the Pharaohs, Nero, Cleopatra, Troy, and dozens of other epics and legends. Each story carried one or more lessons about the causes of human success and failure.

Her grandfather would say again and again, "Wait, wait, my daughter. Love is coming!" And now love had arrived.

Pygma was not someone who sought only her body. He did not want a nurse or nanny to look after him, nor a nursing mother who would bear him children and guard his possessions. He was not looking for someone to provide the pleasures of indulgence and sensuality and to participate in them with him. No—he was none of these things.

He wanted to capture her spirit in the mirror of her own beauty. There was an indescribable power in this kind of conquest, a power through which he could realise his own nature, reveal her hidden talents, draw strength from her, keep his heart joyful, and preserve the health of his spirit. A healthy body, he believed, dwells in a joyful heart and a healthy soul.

He wanted to remember her love and the manifestations of her beauty and affection, so that neither grief nor sorrow would overwhelm him. In the memory of her name and presence, he could carve through a mountain until he reached the stone itself, then shape the stone into a statue. Through his love for her, he could create joy and beauty and leave something behind as a lasting remembrance.

He wanted their love to become an example, so that whenever lovers wished to speak of love and fidelity, they would mention their names. He wanted both her spiritual needs and her physical needs to be fulfilled, allowing her to reach her full potential and to do good. He wanted to attain a kind of immortality, rise above the ordinary, leave a good name behind, and surpass others in his efforts to bring happiness to them.

Electra had understood this. Among her countless suitors, he was the finest candidate. Everything therefore depended upon a loving heart and fidelity to a promise and a pledge. All other matters were secondary, because neither of them lacked anything essential: they had the love of parents, sisters, and family, as well as art, talent, creativity, work, effort, perseverance, and an iron will to advance through life.

Electra's Appearance

Electra had Persian ancestry. Her mother was Persian, her father Roman, and she herself was Greek. She could play the santur, the harp, and the qanun, and she loved Persian carpets. In addition to her extraordinary beauty, she was devoted to learning, books, and wisdom.

The Explanation of Reason and Love

Passages from the Book

Electra, too, rose to her feet and spoke:

"What has happened to you, young man? What troubles you? Have you become love-mad? You have elevated an earthly woman to a divine station and, in the heavens of your imagination, raised me to the rank of Venus, Mithra, Anahita, and Aphrodite. Do you not think that they might punish you for this? Come down from the heavens to the earth, my lord, and tell me: how do you see my earthly, mortal self?"

"No, kind Electra. You are the sum of all the goddesses of the earth, a radiance from the goddess of the heavens. Whoever praises you deserves admiration, not punishment."

Love is a pool of clear water in which anyone stained by the swamp of sin may become clean.

"I do not believe that anyone could love a goddess of the heavens as you do."

"Any tree stump like me, if it possesses even the faintest spark, catches fire in the breeze of your presence."

"Every heart that turns towards your threshold is seized by the attraction of love."

"Your love is captivating—thousands of times more powerful than a magnet."

"O you who have captured my heart!"

"May my sin be the praise and celebration I offer you, for you yourself are innocence embodied."

"May every stained heart be purified in the clear waters of the pure spring of love."

"You are the direction towards which my heart turns."

"Ah, beautiful lady!"

"When you wear a small green headscarf, tied in a little knot beneath your chin, you appear before my eyes in one way."

"Your hair is like golden threads emerging from beneath green leaves."

"And when you wear a blue head covering, tied in little knots beneath your chin, you are like a shining moon lost in the blue of the sky."

"A light radiates from your moonlike face."

"To my eyes, your hair is not merely hair or fibre. It is gold itself, more precious than gold."

"How strange that women spend their lives searching for gold, while they themselves are mines of gold and treasures of precious jewels."

"The lasso of your long hair is long life, and your lips are delightful pleasure."

"Long life and delightful pleasure have become intertwined."

"The sum of these opposites has become possible in your being."

"You are a symbol of the soul's eternity and the fleeting meteor of passing life."

"O captivating beloved, I am drowning in the sea of my passion for you, lost in the contemplation of you."

"I have become more love-mad than those possessed by spirits."

Electra's eyes sparkled as she heard these words. Standing with all her dignity, she burst into loud laughter. Tears of joy appeared at the corners of her eyes. Her laughter seemed to echo through the room, and in the same delighted mood she said:

"Pygma, what an extraordinary creature you are!"

"I wonder how your other rivals in love have ever managed to escape the trap of your words."

"You are the undisputed master of exaggeration."

"You are the sovereign of words and the storyteller of love. Such eloquence and rhetoric! Astonishing!"

She imitated his voice and repeated:

"With a little green covering upon your head, you are the green of green meadows; with a blue covering upon your head, you become the blue of the sky!"

Laughing, she continued, "What else, then? Should I go and buy headscarves in every colour so that you can describe me, O bold sculptor?"

"I must find out how I appear to you in a red covering—and what about a white one?"

"What a remarkable creature you are, you fearless sculptor!"

Electra was happy and laughing as she continued:

"What statue are you talking about? You are sculpting my spirit! Go on, then. How else do you expect me to appear in your eyes? Tell me."

"Who am I? Where have I come from, and why have I come into this world?" Her final words were spoken with a more serious expression.

Pygma continued:

"O honoured lady, your being is formed from a substance called woman—a substance from which all the virtuous women of the ages have been shaped."

"You are a symbol of love and fidelity."

"It was through seeing you that my eyes learned to see."

"I have fallen into this affliction and captivity."

"A captivity unlike any other."

"I have become a Majnun."

"Not merely because of your indescribable beauty, but because of your gentle and dignified spirit, your manners, your calmness, and your respectfulness."

"You are a world of breezes and the tranquillity of a lake."

"In the evenings and at dawn, when not a single wave disturbs the lake's surface, you are the herald of morning at daybreak and the herald of night at twilight."

"Morning and night have become intertwined within you."

"You yourself are a dream—a dream that can lull a person to sleep even beneath the full blaze of daylight, carrying the mind into a world of imagination."

"Ah, beautiful lady, you are the very essence of dreams and imagination."

"You drag this wretched man not merely to the border of madness, but into the abyss of madness itself."

"A madman who has broken free of his chains, while all the other madmen offer him advice." — Rumi

"When love is formed from perfection and wisdom, it is reason set ablaze."

"It is immense delight."

How Can Love Become Universal?

One day, Pygma asked her, "Tell me, dear Electra: how can love be made universal?"

Electra replied, "That is a difficult question. Perhaps it cannot truly be answered in the real world. How can love spread across a world where murder, slaughter, slavery, the violation of other people's rights, violence, and lust run rampant?

"How can love and affection be spread when women—the manifestations of the heavenly goddess—are attacked and violated, sold in the marketplace of the world, and forced into prostitution against their will?

"How can tenderness and love flourish when people have not yet forgotten the violence and savagery of the Pharaohs, Nero, Qin Shi Huang, and the other blood-soaked figures of history?"

Electra continued, "Yet I have mentioned China. In the realm of stories and legends, we can find signs of how love might become a force that reaches across the world."

The Journey of Love

In this way, through telling stories with increasingly complex social themes, Electra seeks to bring Pygmalion's virtue and wisdom to their fullest expression. In truth, she is another version of Cratia, continuing the work of helping Pygmalion reach a higher level of maturity. Through herself and her love stories, she carries him towards the heavens, only to leave him there in a state of wonder.

The love between Electra and Pygmalion represents maturity in both physical and spiritual terms. No veil remains between these two beautiful souls. What obstacle could still stand in their way?

There is no conflict between their families, nor between the two lovers themselves. No social, ethnic, or cultural barrier stands before them. It is as though the clouds, the moon, and the heavens themselves have begun to dance together, determined to lead these two lovers to the home of happiness.

What events lie waiting in the vast and level landscape of their love? What will happen, and what will become of them? It is now for you, the reader, to discover the answers by reading the story of Electra and Pygmalion.

Who, in your own life, has Electra been? What image of Electra—imagined or real—do you carry in your mind? What memories of an Electra do you have in your life? How do you imagine Electra, this symbol of reason and love, should be?

Write down your thoughts and send them to me. And to all the Electras of the world: has a Pygmalion ever crossed your path? What was he like? If you wish, write your story and send it to me.
