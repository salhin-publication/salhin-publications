And This Is the Book of Love

Galatea and Pygmalion

Author: Jalal al-Din Valiallah

Part Four — Act Four of the Play

The Story of Roya

Part Four: The Story of Roya

Regardless of what happens to Pygma's beloved women, are they all still with him while his eager eyes search for yet another beloved?

What mattered was that he was now caught in Roya's dream.

Excerpts from the Story of Roya

High above the City Hall hung a large painted fabric banner. It showed a beautiful woman who was, in fact, Roya. Before her, a man knelt with both hands extended, holding out a three-dimensional crimson symbol of a heart shaped like a purple leaf. It meant that he was offering the woman his heart. In large letters, the words read: “Marriage: Rebirth Again and Again.” This was the title of the play in which Roya performed at the city theatre.

The Role of Roya

Roya embodied dreams and fantasies. Her figure was unlike that of any woman or girl Pygma had ever seen. She had a full figure, with a prominent bust, and her arms, thighs, and calves appeared fuller as well. Yet her innocent expression and beautiful, fair complexion had a moonlit, dreamlike quality that seemed to lull the observer into a dream. She was the very embodiment of sleep and dreams.

Roya came from a very wealthy family of jewelers. Her face was fair and round, with a slightly oval shape. Her eyes were large and black as the night. Two slender, arched eyebrows, curving toward the sides of her face, framed them with a smooth, marble-like sheen, as though her face were a delicate, glossy eggshell, slightly rounded.

Her almond-shaped eyes were not particularly prominent. Their short but noticeable, sparse lashes had been made more visible with a line of black kohl. It was as if those sparse lashes did not wish to cover the broad whites of her eyes, allowing the large, dark pupils at their centers to stand out even more.

Her nose and mouth were finely shaped. The openings and contours of her nostrils were rounded and elegant. The fair skin above the edges of her nose was especially noticeable, extending upward between her eyes and toward the middle of her eyebrows. It seemed that, apart from the nostrils themselves, the rest of the nose almost disappeared into the space between her eyes. The width of the outer edges of her nose was about equal to the distance between the beginnings of her eyebrows.

At a proportionate distance below her nose were her lips, small and relatively thin. The slight fullness of her lower lip, where the corners of her mouth met her cheeks, created a darker hollow that made the subtle curve at the center of the lip appear more pronounced. Her lips were faintly red, almost pale. She wore no makeup; everything about her face was natural.

Pygma, who found it difficult to put his feelings into words, finally spoke to his mother and sister:

“Roya is beautiful, lovable, sincere, simple, and unpretentious. She is warm-hearted, honest, humble, affectionate, and full of kindness. Unlike aristocratic girls, who can be haughty, proud, ambitious, and selfish, attached to their own wealth and that of their families, and accustomed to looking down on others, she is playful, cheerful, lively, and straightforward.

“From the very beginning, it was as though she already knew me, as though I had always been and still were a member of her family—as though I had been her playmate. She drew me into her games, was happy with me, and insists that we continue. Yet this makes me uneasy. Could there be an obstacle?”

The love between Roya and Pygma was taking shape in the art hall, along its sidewalks, and in the jewelry shop and showroom belonging to Roya's father. In truth, it had already taken shape. Roya had fallen in love with Pygma, and he, in turn, was captivated by the spell of her piercing black eyes.

What obstacle could there be now? Who else might be involved? Surely, with good fortune and blessings, they would soon be united. But did they actually reach one another? Or did they not? After they were united, did their feelings grow cold? Is Roya still waiting for Pygma, and Pygma for her, as they wonder what the future will bring?

At this stage in Pygma's development, Roya offered him another experience of perfection in love and beauty: a new and unrepeatable manifestation of beauty, a uniquely sculptural form, with a crystalline figure and eyes as black as jet.

Pygma was moving toward an awakening to other incomparable expressions of love and beauty. His soul was gaining wisdom: the world was vast, and countless forms of beauty lay hidden within it. Roya revealed to him another meaning of the being called woman.

But what other events lay ahead? Those belong to the complete story of the love between Roya and Pygma—a story you may read in full if you wish.

Tell me, what image of Roya do you carry in your mind? What mental picture—or real-life impression—do you have of her? Has there been, or is there now, a Roya in your life? Share your story and the image you hold of her, whether imagined or real.

Has there also been a Pygmalion who crossed the paths of beautiful dreamers? If you wish, share your own experiences of love with me.
