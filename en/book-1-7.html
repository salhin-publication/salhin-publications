Part Seven

The Love Story of Galatea and Pygmalion

Act Seven

Pygmalion is in love with Electra, lost and bewildered in his passion for her. Nothing can draw him away from his love. He spends his days and nights with Electra, and in this state of wonder and confusion, he carves her graceful figure and beautiful face into marble. He places these statues in another alcove of his palace hall, and many others throughout the city squares, in courtyards and enclosures, and in the halls of magnificent palaces and mansions. He carves these figures with love.

He is still in love with Electra when another, sudden passion arrives. He dreams of a golden-belted prince riding a white horse down from the mountains and rocky slopes of Paphos. The prince rides through the city square, urging his white horse into a powerful leap and a loud neigh, while casting a gaze filled with the burning heat of love. In the dream, he tells Pygmalion, “I am Galatea.” As he rides away from the city square, he calls out, “I am your Galatea. Find me!” Then he disappears in the same direction from which he came, fading from sight.

Pygmalion suddenly awakens from his sweet dream, but Galatea is nowhere beside him. He has found her so beautiful, and her eyes so full of love, that he rises, goes to his workshop, and begins to carve her from the purest marble he possesses.

Pygmalion now becomes the very embodiment of the song of the golden-belted prince. From that moment on, every day and every time he strikes the statue with his chisel, he sings the song and repeats its verses:

At dawn, I saw in my dream a prince with a golden belt, Seated upon a white horse, coming down the mountain slopes. He rode away, and his gaze set my heart aflame; He rode away, and his gaze set my heart aflame. If only my heart could be revealed, if these tear-filled eyes could become a sea, When fortune finally smiles on me, the one from my dream will appear. Perhaps you are the prince of my dreams, Perhaps you are the one who visits me in my sleep. You... Ah... Suddenly, I awoke from my sweet dream; I could no longer see him beside me, ah! Grief has brought my soul to the edge of my lips; Every day and every night, I wait for him, ah! At dawn, I saw in my dream a prince with a golden belt, Seated upon a white horse, coming down the mountain slopes. He rode away, and his gaze set my heart aflame; He rode away, and his gaze set my heart aflame.

Songwriter: Bijan Taraqqi; Composer: Homayoun Khorram. English rendering of the Persian lyrics.

Day and night, singing as he works, Pygmalion sets out to create another work of art: flawless, exquisitely delicate, and magnificent. He carries it to his palace and sleeps each night at its feet, hoping that he might see her again in a dream.

His tearful eyes and passionate love for the statue he has carved, together with the silk garments and jewels he places upon her, catch Berlian's attention. She realizes that he has once again become a lovesick, enchanted, and utterly devoted man.

What happens next? What becomes of his legendary woman? Will Venus look upon the purity of Pygmalion's love and devotion, take pity on him, and

Breathe life into Galatea and bring her to life?

These are the events that follow. If you wish to discover how the story ends, you may continue after reading the earlier parts. Let the ending of this beautiful tale remain mysterious and veiled for now, so that you, too, may read the book and explore its many love stories with excitement and passion.

I look forward to hearing your stories and reading your own tales of love. Perhaps one of them will inspire another novel, a story that we may create together with your help. Perhaps?!!
